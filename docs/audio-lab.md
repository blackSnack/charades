# Party Deck Audio Lab — Cozy Party v0.1

Art direction: warm, relaxed, instrumental lounge outside play; cheerful, light, speech-friendly music during play. Every existing deck skin gets its own instrumentation, not a different game rule. Shared major/seventh-chord vocabulary and a recurring phrase shape; multiple grooves and keys.

The twelve EPs contain lounge/play arrangements (16 bars, loopable) and a twelve-event sound palette including four difficulty flips. The browser renders procedural instrument samples and score to an OfflineAudioContext on explicit audition; no external model, samples, or paid API are needed. All scores and synthesis definitions are editable in score.ts. This is a composition and sound palette prototype, not a sampled studio recording or final master. It uses no third-party recordings. No audio is played in the production game until listening approval. Only one preview plays; stop, return, selection change and document hiding cancel playback; rendering completion is guarded by a generation token.

Selections are per deck theme and audition scene; review notes persist locally and can be exported as JSON. They do not change game preferences. Human review: compare lounge/play pair, warmth, rhythm, instrumentation, fatigue over multiple loops, speech audibility, event recognition, and mobile speaker sound. Check real iOS/PWA playback before promotion.

References for future production (not runtime dependencies): owoevan-dev/music-skill (composition, arrangement and sampled instruments); GhazaGG/Game-Sound-Effect-for-Claude (layered events); JimCline/game-audio-kit (audition and review). Adopt sampled instruments and dedicated foley after direction approval, subject to licensing and human-managed build environment.

## V2 — real sampled instruments and in-game evaluation

Use written melodic phrases, fewer active layers, natural note attacks and releases, controlled registers, speech-friendly levels, and short tactile event cues. Eleven CC0 Meta Piano and Nylon Guitar samples are vendored under public/audio-samples/v2, verified against upstream Git blob hashes. Bass, wood, brush, marimba and bell remain synthesized; card slide/place use two CC0 Kenney card-handling recordings with synthetic fallback. No claim of studio mastering. Reference: https://github.com/owoevan-dev/music-skill and https://github.com/GhazaGG/Game-Sound-Effect-for-Claude. Audio profiles are separate from visual skins; apply a candidate or selected per-scene picks, disable at any time. Default is silent until explicit application. Game effects use committed reducer events; final-turn cue takes priority over repeated correct+flip; normal Solo next plays difficulty flip. Lab stops production audio. Human listening and iOS device validation remain necessary. Modern browser Ogg/Vorbis decoding is required for sampled music.

## V3 — balance and voice leading

12 EP IDs and existing profiles retained. A melody and harmony are now written together per bar in `arrangement.ts`. All melodic pitches are chord tones, harmony sits below the melody and uses sparse two-note gestures, no second foreground voice. Note events cannot spill across a harmony boundary. Active-window RMS is measured independently on rendered stems; lead/harmony/bass/rhythm targets are .040/.014/.011/.0045 (approximately 0/-9/-11/-19 dB). Final gain is common peak protection only, not a second automatic loudness boost. Different stem filters soften competing attacks. Scene changes fade through silence to avoid uncorrelated chords overlapping. Frequent SFX are quieter and predominantly unpitched; no fast BGM gain pumping. These engineering rules are not a substitute for human listening; final music quality requires audition.
